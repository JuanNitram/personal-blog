Case study 01 · Tithely · Sermonly

A Notion for churches, built from zero.

I designed, built and maintained Sermonly from the first line of code: a workspace where churches write and organize their content, with AI and Bible tools built in. It started as a desktop app, grew to the web on the same codebase, and later got its own mobile app.

Role
Designer, builder, maintainer
Users
~8,000 registered
Paying
~10% subscribe
Platforms
Electron · Web · Ionic

Churches write a lot. Their tools didn't help.

Pastors and church teams produce a lot of written content, and they need to research Scripture, find the right words and keep everything organized. Sermonly gives them one workspace, a bit like Notion, but made for churches.

Today it has around 8,000 registered users, and roughly 1 in 10 is a paying subscriber.

The smart parts come from outside.

  • AI features. AI integrations built into the writing experience to help users draft and work with their content.
  • Wording help. Integration with wording APIs to look up synonyms and find the right word without leaving the page.
  • Bible verses. Verse search through Bible sources like Bible Gateway, so Scripture comes straight into the document.
  • Stripe subscriptions. Paid plans run on Stripe subscriptions: sign-up, recurring billing and access to premium features.

Desktop, then web, then mobile.

  1. Electron first. Sermonly started as an Electron desktop app.
  2. Web on the same codebase. We extended it to the web reusing the same codebase, so features shipped to both at once.
  3. Mobile with Ionic. Later we built the mobile app with Ionic as a new codebase, designed for how people use phones.

Owning a product end to end.

Because I built Sermonly from zero, every decision was mine to make and to live with: the data model, the integrations, billing and three platforms. It taught me to design for the version after this one. Reusing the codebase for the web paid off. Choosing a new one for mobile was the right call too.
