Case study 08 · Podcast SaaS

A podcast platform, and my first Kubernetes project

A platform where podcasters manage their shows and episodes, published to every major player through generated feeds. When the simple setup it started on stopped being enough, I took on moving it to Kubernetes with autoscaling. It was my first hands-on Kubernetes project, and I learned it as I went.

Industry
Podcasting
Role
Tech lead and project manager, also writing code
Team
Led a team of 3–4 developers
Stack
RSS/XML feeds · Kubernetes (first project)
Built
By hand, pre-AI

My role and responsibilities

  • Led a small team of 3–4 developers: assigning work, reviewing code and unblocking people.
  • Acted as project manager: planning, scope, priorities and day-to-day communication with the client.
  • Owned the architecture and the main technical decisions.
  • Stayed hands-on, implementing core features myself.
  • Took on the infrastructure migration personally.

Key decisions

  • Generate one standard feed per show. An XML feed that Spotify, Apple, Google Podcasts and any other player can read.
  • Move from a load balancer to Kubernetes. A fixed set of servers behind a load balancer stopped being enough when traffic became less predictable.
  • Add autoscaling. So capacity could follow demand.

What we built

  • Show and episode management. The core product.
  • Feed generation. For every major podcast player.
  • Kubernetes setup with autoscaling. Running in production.

The hard part

I'm not a Kubernetes expert. This was my first real project with it, and I learned most of it on the way. I probably didn't follow every best practice, but I got it running in production.

What I learned

It gave me a practical understanding of how pods, services and autoscaling behave, and what I would do differently next time.
