Case study 08 · Studeo

From one load balancer to Kubernetes.

Studeo let podcasters manage their shows and episodes in one place, and published them everywhere people listen. When the simple setup it started on stopped being enough, I moved it to Kubernetes with autoscaling and ran it in production.

Context

Publish once, play everywhere.

Podcasters used Studeo to manage their podcasts and episodes. For each show, the platform generated an XML feed that could be submitted to Spotify, Apple, Google Podcasts and any other player that reads podcast feeds. Listeners never see Studeo, but every play depends on that feed being correct and online.

Architecture

The infrastructure had to grow up.

Studeo started on a very simple architecture: a load balancer in front of a fixed set of servers. That works until traffic stops being predictable. I took on moving it to Kubernetes with autoscaling, so capacity follows demand instead of a guess.

What I built
  1. Show and episode managementThe core product: podcasters create and manage their podcasts and episodes.
  2. Feed generationGenerated the podcast XML feed that streaming platforms read to list and play each show.
  3. Kubernetes migrationMoved production from the load-balancer setup to a Kubernetes cluster.
  4. AutoscalingSet up autoscaling so the platform adds capacity when load goes up and releases it when it drops.
Honest take

Not textbook. In production.

This was my first serious Kubernetes project, and I had to learn a lot of it on the way. I probably didn't follow every best practice. But I put it into production and it worked, and that taught me more than any course: how pods, services and scaling behave under real traffic, and what to do better next time.
