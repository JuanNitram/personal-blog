Case study 08 · Studeo

A podcast platform, and my first Kubernetes project.

Studeo let podcasters manage their shows and episodes in one place, and published them everywhere people listen. When the simple setup it started on stopped being enough, I took on moving it to Kubernetes with autoscaling. It was my first hands-on Kubernetes project, and I learned it as I went.

Publish once, play everywhere.

Podcasters used Studeo to manage their podcasts and episodes. For each show, the platform generated an XML feed that could be submitted to Spotify, Apple, Google Podcasts and any other player that reads podcast feeds. Listeners never see Studeo, but every play depends on that feed being correct and online.

Learning Kubernetes on a real project.

Studeo started on a very simple architecture: a load balancer in front of a fixed set of servers. That works until traffic stops being predictable. I took on moving it to Kubernetes with autoscaling, so capacity could follow demand. I had not worked with Kubernetes before, so a big part of the job was learning it.

What I built

  1. Show and episode management. The core product: podcasters create and manage their podcasts and episodes.
  2. Feed generation. Generated the podcast XML feed that streaming platforms read to list and play each show.
  3. Kubernetes migration. Moved production from the load-balancer setup to a Kubernetes cluster.
  4. Autoscaling. Set up autoscaling so the platform adds capacity when load goes up and releases it when it drops.

Not textbook, but it worked.

I'm not a Kubernetes expert. This was my first real project with it, and I learned most of it on the way. I probably didn't follow every best practice. But I got it running in production, and it gave me a practical understanding of how pods, services and autoscaling behave, and what I would do differently next time.
