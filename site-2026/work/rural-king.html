Case study 07 · Rural King · via BuildOnline

A store scanner, rendered on the server.

Through BuildOnline we built Rural King a replacement for the internal app their staff use on in-store handheld devices, the kind that look like a POS. Scan a product, check stock here and in other stores across the US, and work through your task list. The client required Laravel and Hotwire, so every screen is rendered on the server inside native iOS and Android wrappers.

A tool every store depends on.

Rural King staff carry handheld devices on the store floor. The existing internal app on those devices was being replaced, and the new one had to cover the daily work of store employees: knowing what a product is, how much of it there is, and where else it can be found.

Features

  1. Product scanner. The main feature: scan a product in the store to bring up everything about it.
  2. Stock availability. See and manage the stock available in the current store.
  3. Stock in other stores. Check which other Rural King stores across the United States have the product in stock.
  4. Inventory tools. A whole set of features around managing stock inside the stores.
  5. Task manager. A built-in task manager that syncs with a separate API, so each employee has their own backlog of tasks on the same device.

The constraint was the design.

The client set a clear requirement: build it with Laravel and Hotwire. That shaped everything. Instead of a native app with its own client-side logic, the screens are rendered server-side and shown inside thin iOS and Android wrappers on the devices.

Native feel, server brain.

With Hotwire, the logic lives on the server and the native shell mostly displays it. That keeps one codebase in charge and lets a server change reach every device. The challenge is making server-rendered screens work like a tool people use all day on the store floor, with the scanner at the center, inside two native wrappers.

It's the same kind of problem I met in Breeze Worship, where we bridged Flutter with HTML and JavaScript: web content living inside a native app, and making the seam invisible.
