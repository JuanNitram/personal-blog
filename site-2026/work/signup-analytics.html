Case study 04 · Tithely · Analytics

Making signup data worth trusting.

Before marketing could switch to production analytics, someone had to prove the numbers were real. I audited the whole signup pipeline by hand, found three silent bugs, fixed them in the right order, and only then shipped the cutover.

Role
Engineering lead, hands-on
Org
Tithely · EPD
When
2026
Stack
GA4 · PostHog · GTM · SQL

Two tools, one funnel, no single truth.

Signup analytics ran through Google Tag Manager into GA4 for marketing, and PostHog for product. The GTM signup events were still pointed at a staging GA4 property. The plan was simple on paper: point them at the real production property so marketing reports reflect real signups.

The risk was not the switch itself. The risk was switching on numbers nobody had checked. Once phantom users land in marketing-facing reports, they drive spend and decisions, and they are very hard to take back out.

Audit first. Rank by what can't be undone.

I ran a production QA pass across the signup instrumentation and audited the GTM container by hand: every tag and trigger fan-out, duplicate tags, and the custom-dimension slot budget. I reconciled the backend and frontend event taxonomies so both tools used the same names for the same moments.

Then I ranked every issue by irreversibility, not by how loud it was. Anything that would permanently corrupt history went first.

Three bugs nobody had noticed.

  • Robots signing up. Signup events were inflated by load-balancer health checks and a security scanner hitting the flow. Cutting over as-is would have poured phantom users straight into marketing reports. Fixed before the cutover.
  • A 3.3× over-count. A KYC-submitted event fired about 3.3 times too often. The event-mapping gate checked a state ("is submitted") instead of a transition ("just became submitted"), so it fired again on every later update.
  • Attribution silently lost for everyone. Campaign attribution ($initial_utm_source) was missing for 100% of persons. The cause was one missing setting, person_profiles: 'always', in the PostHog client config. As the most irreversible issue, it went first.

Shipping

  1. Delay on purpose. Held the GA4 cutover until the inflation bug was fixed, so the production property would start clean.
  2. Author the change myself. Wrote and published the production GTM container version that moved signup events from staging GA4 to the real property, and registered the custom dimensions it needed.
  3. Verify with live data. Watched live event counts after publish instead of trusting the change. I also caught and corrected my own misread of the GA4 Realtime window before it became a wrong claim.
  4. Don't stop at a 403. When the PostHog billing API refused access, I worked out the cost-tier picture directly with SQL on the event data.

Marketing got real numbers on day one.

Signup analytics now land in the production GA4 property, without bot traffic in them. The over-counted event and the lost attribution were traced to exact root causes in code and config, not patched over in a dashboard.

The bigger win is the habit: check every claim against live data before acting on it, including my own.
