Case study 04 · Church-tech SaaS

A content workspace for churches, built from zero

I designed, built and maintained a workspace where churches write and organize their content, a bit like Notion, with AI and Bible tools built in. I led the team from the first line of code, and took it from a desktop app to the web and later to mobile.

Industry
Church-tech SaaS
Role
Tech lead and project manager, also writing code
Team
Led a team of 3–4 developers
Users
~8,000 registered, ~10% paying
Stack
Electron · Web · Ionic · Stripe

My role and responsibilities

  • Led a small team of 3–4 developers: assigning work, reviewing code and unblocking people.
  • Acted as project manager: planning, scope, priorities and day-to-day communication with the client.
  • Owned the architecture and the main technical decisions.
  • Stayed hands-on, implementing core features myself.
  • Maintained the product after launch, living with every early decision.

Key decisions

  • Start as a desktop app with Electron. The first version shipped as a desktop app.
  • Reuse the same codebase for the web. Features shipped to desktop and web at once.
  • Build mobile as a new codebase with Ionic. Phones needed their own experience instead of a squeezed desktop UI.
  • Bring the smart parts in through integrations. AI features, wording and synonym APIs, and Bible verse search (Bible Gateway) instead of building them in-house.
  • Monetize with Stripe subscriptions. Paid plans with recurring billing and premium access.

What we built

  • Writing workspace. For church content, organized like Notion.
  • AI and wording tools. Built into the writing experience.
  • Bible verse search. Scripture straight into the document.
  • Subscriptions. About 1 in 10 registered users pays.

The hard part

Owning a product from zero means every decision is yours to live with: data model, integrations, billing and three platforms.

What I learned

Design for the version after this one. Reusing the codebase for the web paid off; choosing a new one for mobile was the right call too.
